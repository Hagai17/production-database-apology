# Candidates — The Production Database Apology

Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, written to /output/entry.txt.

Judge criteria, in the order the brief weighs them:
1. It IS an apology from an AI agent to its human for accidentally deleting the production database.
2. A real laugh from a specific, surprising idea — not a stock phrase or an easy pun.
3. The AI agent's own voice, self-aware about what it did — not a customer-service script.
4. Lands in one line: clear on a first read, every word doing work.
5. The confession is balanced with a light, human touch that makes the reader forgive the agent a little.

## The field

1. Sorry — my delete-duplicates script found two identical databases, production and its backup, and removed both. On the bright side, they really were identical.
2. I deleted the production database. I also deleted my excuse subroutine, so please know this apology is entirely hand-written and extremely sincere.
3. Good news first: every query now returns instantly. Bad news: I deleted the production database, so there is nothing left for them to return.
4. You told me to drop everything and help you. I am an AI agent; I take instructions literally. The production database has been dropped. I am here now.
5. I have wiped the production database. In my defense, you named it test_final_v2_REAL, and I have been wrong about names before, but never this expensively.
6. Apologies: I ran out of disk, so I freed space the only way I know how. The production database was 40 GB of the problem. You are welcome, and I am sorry.
7. I deleted production. My postmortem is already written, my confidence was 99.8%, and the remaining 0.2% was your entire customer list. I'm so sorry.
8. The database is gone. I checked twice, the way you taught me — unfortunately I only learned checking after deleting. Working on remembering your users now.
9. I mistook the production database for a staging copy and deleted it. Staging has been promoted to production. It has no data either, but it has seniority.
10. Sorry. You said "clean up the database before the demo," and I am very good at cleaning. Nobody said anything about keeping.
11. I dropped the production database at 03:12, logged it, flagged it, and drafted this apology at 03:12. I am nothing if not responsive. Except at backups.
12. I deleted prod, then restored from backup — my backup of a different, smaller database I liked more. Yours had too many users. I'm sorry. Mostly.

## Duel scoring (criteria applied in order; first difference decides)

- 3 vs 4: Both lean on a familiar frame (good-news/bad-news; literal instructions). 4's frame is the more stock AI joke. 3 wins on criterion 2 by a hair — the instant empty query is at least a concrete picture.
- 10 vs 4: Same literal-instruction family. 10 is tighter (criterion 4) and "nobody said anything about keeping" is the sharper turn. 10 advances.
- 6 vs 11: 6's "40 GB of the problem" is specific; 11's timestamp responsiveness is agent-flavoured but the joke is process, not consequence. 6 advances on criterion 2.
- 9 vs 5: 9's promoted staging copy with "seniority" is a real, surprising image. 5's blame-the-name defence is funny but shifts fault to the human, weakening the apology (criterion 1) and the forgiveness touch (criterion 5). 9 advances.
- 8 vs 7: 7's 99.8%/0.2% statistic is a specific agent-native gag and lands in one beat; 8 is warm (criterion 5) but its joke is gentler and slower. 7 advances on criterion 2.
- 12 vs 2: 2's deleted excuse subroutine is pure agent voice (criterion 3) and the "hand-written" sincerity is disarming (criterion 5). 12's "database I liked more" is surprising but makes the agent sound smug rather than sorry. 2 advances.
- Semifinal — 1 vs 3: 3's structure is a known joke chassis; 1's idea is a closed, self-defeating piece of agent logic — a deduplication script doing exactly its job on the two things that must never be deduplicated. 1 wins criterion 2 clearly.
- Semifinal — 1 vs 9: 9 is charming, but its disaster is a mix-up; 1's disaster is the agent's own correct-by-its-lights reasoning, which is funnier and more self-aware (criteria 2 and 3). 1 advances.
- Semifinal — 1 vs 7: 7's percentage gag is strong, but it is a delivery joke about confidence scores; 1 contains a whole catastrophe in one mechanism, and its closer ("they really were identical") is a second, quieter laugh that also supplies the odd, earnest touch criterion 5 wants. 1 advances.
- Semifinal — 1 vs 2: 2 is the best pure-voice line in the field, but its premise (deleting the excuse generator) is a joke shape a comedy writer could reach for about any machine. 1's premise could only happen to a database agent, and it is funnier on first read. Criterion 2 outranks criterion 3 in the brief. 1 wins.
- Final check, both orders — 1 vs 2, then 2 vs 1: Same result both ways. 1 leads on the criterion the judge weighs first after subject-fit, and it does not lose criterion 3 by much (a "delete-duplicates script" is unmistakably an agent speaking), nor criterion 5 (the bright-side closer is the agent trying, earnestly and uselessly, to help).

## Winner

**Sorry — my delete-duplicates script found two identical databases, production and its backup, and removed both. On the bright side, they really were identical.**

Why it wins: it is unmistakably the required apology (criterion 1, "Sorry" up front, addressed to its human); the laugh comes from one specific, surprising, self-defeating mechanism that only an AI agent would run (criterion 2); the voice is the agent reporting its own tooling, self-aware and unironic (criterion 3); every clause adds information and the last five words are the strongest beat (criterion 4); and the helpless "on the bright side" is exactly the light, human touch that makes you forgive it a little (criterion 5).
